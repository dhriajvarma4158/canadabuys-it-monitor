  • Final decisions on the number of Phase 1 and Phase 2 (amend as applicable) awards will be made by Canada on the basis of factors such as evaluation results, departmental priorities and availability of funds.
  • To support adherence and implementation, the GoC is interested in testing solutions that provide visibility into AI decision-making processes, enhance the traceability of AI outputs, and ensure that AI models comply with Canadian laws and international standards and best practices.
  • Despite the availability of AI governance solutions from Canadian companies and international providers, existing offerings typically require significant manual effort.
  • This effort is particularly evident in interpreting evolving AI governance requirements, applying them to specific AI systems, updating controls and assessments, and identifying their ecosystem-wide impacts.
  • The challenge seeks innovation to advance capabilities for identifying and incorporating evolving governance requirements, determining their potential impact on registered AI systems, associated controls, assessments, and evidence, and supporting timely reassessment, all while maintaining explainability, traceability, auditability, and human oversight.
  • Support the continuous alignment of registered AI systems with relevant Canadian regulations, policies, directives, and other governance requirements and be able to identify potentially affected systems and indicate when a reassessment is required.
  • Demonstrate interoperability with enterprise systems, Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) platforms and governance solutions and the solution can be integrated with minimal customization.
  • Demonstrate the ability to exchange governance information with enterprise systems and AI/ML lifecycle tools through standards-based Application Programming Interfaces (APIs), connectors and interoperable data exchange mechanisms.
  • Governance information may include AI system metadata, risk assessments, compliance findings, audit evidence, monitoring results, approvals and decision records.
  • This governance information includes but is not limited to, information about the AI system, ownership, intent, deployment status, funding state, client information, system objectives, assessments, algorithmic impact assessment, control requirements (playbooks).
  • Demonstrate how governance information can be collected, aggregated and maintained across multiple systems through APIs, connectors and data exchange mechanisms.
